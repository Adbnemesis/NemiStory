My whole class watched my old YouTube videos during computer period. Which is a very efficient way to make someone regret learning how to upload.

And no, it wasn't this channel. This was a much older one. With my actual face in it. Already a terrible start.

I think this was around 2015, when Dubsmash was popular. You'd take an audio clip, lip-sync to it, and act like you'd just delivered the performance of your life.

That was me. Face on camera. Full commitment. Absolutely no consideration for the fact that I might have to remember this later.

And somehow, that channel had around a thousand subscribers. Back then, that felt huge. A thousand people had voluntarily signed up for whatever I was doing.

So I told one friend at school. One. A perfectly reasonable audience size for this information.

Then we had computer period. Computers. Internet. My friend, now equipped with the one piece of information I should have kept to myself.

Bro opened my channel. And played my videos.

The whole class saw them. My face. My lip-syncing. Every expression that I had apparently decided the internet needed.

Everyone was laughing. And I was standing there feeling so stupid. Like, okay. This is who I am to these people now.

Posting publicly had somehow felt less embarrassing than watching people I actually knew watch it. The internet was imaginary. My classmates were right there.

But thinking about it now, I don't think they were being mean. They were laughing, sure. Those videos were funny. That was kind of the point.

And they actually seemed impressed that I had a thousand subscribers. I was busy being embarrassed, so I didn't really make room for that part.

Now it feels kind of cute. Just a kid making videos, taking it seriously, and accidentally getting an audience in computer class.

Eventually, YouTube got stricter, work piled up, and I couldn't give that channel the attention anymore. So I dropped it.

And now, after all of that, here I am. Making YouTube videos again.

This time I've replaced my face with a drawing. Character development.

If my old classmates find this one too... at least they'll already know I'm committed to the bit.
