# NEMI storytime episode start

## Full-episode richness and 1080p review gate

Read `docs/animation/STORYTIME_EPISODE_COLOUR_THEME.md` and inspect the actual Nemi EP06 "How I Animate" movie as the current shared episode colour reference. Use cream paper, dark burgundy/author-specific ink, soft neutral washes, canonical character colours and selective prop/doodle accents. Develop scene richness through authored locations, unique evidence/props, live ink, acting and camera direction. Broad saturated walls, roads, multicolour building blocks or dark full-room fills are not the default. Keep meaningful light/mood inside the restrained paper treatment. This applies to episode animation; promotional thumbnails retain their separately invented palettes.

A full episode needs sustained authored visual storytelling with the **upper-middle balance approved in Nemi EP11 R10**. Keep simple, recognizable backgrounds visible and develop thought-specific props, held/live doodles, poses, expressions, camera actions and audible event-linked SFX sequentially. Maintain one audience focus at a time; a beat can contain several purposeful developments without keeping them all on screen. When the user says there is too much, reduce overlap, density or duration while preserving the scene and performance. When they ask for more, develop the thought rather than permanently accumulating decorations. Keep quiet emotional moments, then resume visual development. See the accepted example and revision guidance in `docs/animation/STORYTIME_REFINEMENT_WORKFLOW.md`; its cue counts are descriptive, not quotas or a template.

Before animation, plan each thought beat's illustration/prop, held or live drawing action, expression/body gesture, camera action, recognizable setting and sound cue or deliberate silence. Use story-specific evidence and visual counterpoints. Mix held art with live sketches, revisions and reveals where they advance the thought. Completed ink stays stable; the surrounding acting and camera carry the changing thought.

Draw from the existing pose, expression and hand vocabulary. Vary emotional states, asymmetric full-body gestures, gaze-led transitions and deliberate camera paths/cuts; do not merely cycle a few recipes or keep almost every shot fixed. Stage backgrounds with purposeful colour/value contrast, layers, scene detail and narrative light. Inspect both arms, wrists and grips across the whole film, especially after the midpoint and around path/attachment ownership boundaries. Never leave an empty gripping hand after a prop goes away. Use recognizable event-linked existing SFX throughout the thought progression and check their actual prominence beneath narration. These are creative decisions, not an effects-per-second quota; keep silence and holds where they earn their place.

Respect the user's requested runtime as a hard limit through every revision. A short brief must not silently become a five-minute episode; tighten writing instead of rushing the approved voice.

Review Nemi's conversational pace and prolonged sentence gaps explicitly. Reuse approved Sohee recordings and choose phrase-specific pitch-preserving tempo, normally 1.08–1.12× for passages that drag, at most 1.15× after listening; slower emotional phrases may stay near 1.00×. Shorten only selected non-emotional gaps with explicit source bounds. Preserve breaths, hesitation and the feeling of worry/gratitude. Rebuild word times, mouths, captions, acting, camera and sound timing from the final edited recording. ADB's clear pacing is a reference for flow, not permission to change Nemi's speaker, identity, pitch, formants or EQ.

**Export the complete episode in 1920×1080 first. Do not render a 4K episode or 4K video proof until the user explicitly says they are satisfied with the current full 1080p version.** Technical checks, a ten-second proof, an integrated excerpt, an agent's own approval, general permission to create a video, and earlier approval of a different cut do not satisfy this gate. Revise and deliver full 1080p versions until that approval arrives. Keep the review pending without asking for premature approval of unfinished work. Any subsequent picture, voice/timing or mix change invalidates prior approval. Static promotional thumbnail masters remain on their separate native-image route.

The shared renderer checks `review/1080P_APPROVAL.json` for numbered-episode 4K exports, binding the user's actual approval quote to the exact current scene, picture/voice input signature and full 1080p preview/render-stamp hashes. Never invent that quote or receipt. Store indices/logs/QA under `review/`. Preserve rejected previews and voices while revising; final-only render cleanup happens after the approved replacement passes checks.

## Thumbnails

For thumbnail work, read [Storytime thumbnails](../../../docs/animation/STORYTIME_THUMBNAILS.md) at `docs/animation/STORYTIME_THUMBNAILS.md`. Use the built-in image-generation tool (`image_gen.imagegen`) by default, following that guide's reference, generation, packaging and review route. The former Godot thumbnail compositor/validator and promotional-layout workflow are historical references, not the current creation route. Use image generation for future thumbnails unless the user explicitly changes that choice.

Inspect `references/thumbnail_ref/thumbnail_set_raora.png`, the approved generated EP10 reference at `nemi/episodes/ep10_my_teacher_used_to_stalk_me/thumbnail/thumbnail.png`, and actual frames/art from the target Nemi episode before generating. Use the shared references for promotional composition and typography, and Nemi's own episode art for character identity: preserve her recognizable face, hair, clothing, proportions and rounded warm ink/fill language. Do not replace her with a generic anime design or another narrator's face.

Follow the approved Raora-inspired treatment: bright white or pale checker backgrounds, close expressive faces, clear cutout edges and thick broad white sans-serif headline type with BLACK/RED/BLACK outline layers. Invent a story-specific visual idea through interaction, comic scale, metaphor, props and composition while keeping the episode's emotional promise truthful. Use one eye-catching headline without smaller captions/labels and a complementary proposed title. Do not invent real injuries/deaths or claim CTR improvement without audience data.

For a new episode's thumbnail pair, create two genuinely distinct concepts unless the user asks for one image; differ in idea/composition, not just colour or wording. After selection, only the chosen image is the current thumbnail. Follow explicit user instructions about preserving, archiving or deleting old alternatives. Save the native generated image, upload JPG, prompt, reference/provenance record and actual QA under the episode's `thumbnail/` as directed by the shared guide. Inspect full and mobile views beside Nemi episode art for headline readability, character recognizability and visual impact. Record actual dimensions and any resizing honestly; a generated illustration is not an exact rig/Godot render, and an upscale is not native 4K.

Thumbnail-only work uses this image route and preserves animation, voice, timing and rig/pose/expression/hand libraries; do not author animation scenes or fabricate animation-preflight receipts. For combined episode/thumbnail work, complete the animation preparation below as well. Episode animation continues through the shared Godot production route and full 1080p user-satisfaction gate.

Work from the repository root containing `tools/storytime/preflight.py`. Read `docs/animation/EPISODE_START.md` for the shared procedure. Nemi uses the warmer, expressive personality and rounded/looser story pen. Read her character bible, acting and dialogue guides selected by the manifest; canonical voice is Sohee.

1. Resolve the requested story and narrator from the user's brief. Use the numbered episode folder `nemi/episodes/epNN_<story_slug>/`; reserve `common/storytime/examples/` for explicit technical/art studies only; preserve the existing episodes, rigs, poses, expression/hand/mouth libraries and canonical voice settings.
2. Start with `python3 tools/storytime/new_scene.py --author nemi --episode <number> --name <story_slug>`. Use the requested episode number, or omit `--episode` to allocate the next unused number. `--study` is only for an explicit isolated study. This makes only a silent placeholder and pending reading record. For an existing new production, use its `preflight.json`; do not overwrite it. If the new folder has no record, initialize it with `python3 tools/storytime/preflight.py init --folder <folder> --authors nemi`.
3. Run `python3 tools/storytime/preflight.py status --folder <folder>`. Read each listed document in order through `read --folder <folder> --document <path> --part <number>`. Read **all** displayed parts into context. Then `ack --folder <folder> --document <path> --note '<how this document changes this episode>'`. Use specific notes, not automated boilerplate or invented evidence of reading. These subcommands all belong to `python3 tools/storytime/preflight.py`.
4. For a guest character, extend with `--authors nemi adb` and read the other skill/added documents. If required features change, extend the feature set. On resume, inspect current notes and `status`; reread changed material or material absent from the current context. Read the actual docs, not filenames or only their headings.
5. Once ready, write the story brief and beat sheet; then author with the shared version-2 stage. Preserve voice identity, choose pace by the phrase, measure final word times, use purposeful held/live drawings and readable framing against real set/object scale. Establish the story location with scenery and relevant props; handwritten place names and live marks do not replace a scene. Choose closer framing only for a specific thought, and author continuous contacts through existing controls. The current workflows supersede historical percentages, procedural idle motion, old APIs and voice audition alternatives.
6. Read the mandatory `docs/animation/COMMON_CAMERA_STAGING.md` through preflight. Save a camera plan linked to measured words/thoughts: portraits, location wides, object inserts, selected reaction cuts and holds. Reframe the entire world; keep character-to-set scale consistent. Review closer face/mouth detail and avoid repeating one fixed wide shot or constant zooming.
7. Read mandatory `docs/animation/COMMON_SFX_SYSTEM.md` through preflight and save an event-linked sound plan. First inspect `common/audio/sfx/root_sfx_inventory.json` for the exact root meme MP3s, then existing category-folder recordings. Use these assets; do not generate SFX or substitute older procedural clips. Validate their paths/provenance with `tools/storytime/sfx_assets.py`. Review every thought beat for recognizable reaction/prop/drawing/transition accents or intentional silence. Measure and audition the exported mix; cue counts and low source gains do not prove audibility.
8. Validate through `validate_scene.py`, render through `render_scene.py`, review a focused proof and a 30–60 second integrated sequence when scope requires it, and record actual visual/audio QA. Keep unperformed checks explicit. Save/push source and small artifacts within the user's authorization; large media remains local. Deliver the complete 1080p review cut first, then wait for explicit user satisfaction before 4K. Save revision/resolution filenames under this episode's `renders/` and the render index under `review/RENDERS.md`; retain voice/source assets and earlier previews until the approved replacement passes checks. The shared renderer accepts the matching numbered episode render folder. Workspace `renders/` is for studies/intermediate media.

For episode animation, do not bypass preparation by using version 1, direct Godot export, low-level schema validation, forged receipts, or changes to the historical-spec exemption list. Preparation is not artistic approval and does not expand authorization to old episodes or rig changes. Use the common manifest as the document list; do not maintain a second stale copy here.
