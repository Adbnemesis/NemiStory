# I Get Attached Way Too Much — script and measured beats

Runtime: 160.400s, hard cap 180s. Canonical Sohee.

## 1. 0.000–14.195s — A friendly conversation becomes a lifetime bond.

I get attached way too much. Someone becomes my friend, and apparently my brain signs a lifetime contract. They were just having a conversation. I'm already planning the reunion.

Focus: Nemi and contract metaphor. Notebook appears, dry portrait, imagined reunion clears. Show her self-awareness immediately.

## 2. 14.195–25.565s — The city trip offers a reunion after years.

Once, I went to a city with my family, and two of my friends lived there. We hadn't met in two or three years. So obviously, I was excited.

Focus: City and hopeful face. Street wide, suitcase, then an excited portrait. Give the anticipation a specific place.

## 3. 25.565–37.580s — She imagines the whole catch-up before it happens.

I asked both of them if we could meet. In my head, we'd catch up, talk about everything, and forget how long it had been. I'd already imagined the whole day.

Focus: Held phone then imagined cafe. Continuous phone grip and a cafe vignette. Let hope create the stakes.

## 4. 37.580–50.060s — Both friends are busy.

Both of them said they were busy. And I just stared at my phone. I came from so far away. You can't take one day off for me? One day?

Focus: Reply evidence then hurt reaction. Two replies replace the imagined day; face then suitcase wide. Respect the disappointment without inventing motives.

## 5. 50.060–64.120s — Understanding other lives does not erase hurt.

That was what I wanted to say. I know people have work, plans, their own lives. But knowing that didn't magically make it stop hurting. I really wanted to see them.

Focus: Quiet face and open hands. Release the phone at a cut, acknowledge their plans, settle. Keep emotional complexity.

## 6. 64.120–76.120s — She wanted to feel missed too.

I think the part that hurt was realizing I'd been looking forward to something they might not have been thinking about at all. I'd missed them. I wanted to feel missed too.

Focus: Empty chairs then portrait. Cafe callback changes meaning, quiet admission. Let the central hurt land.

## 7. 76.120–91.098s — The same unequal energy happened in school.

And this wasn't new. Even in school, I'd text my friends all excited, hoping they'd reply with that same energy. I'd have so much to say. Then the reply would feel so small.

Focus: Outgoing versus incoming message. School corridor wide then message evidence, small underline. Connect the present to the older pattern.

## 8. 91.098–104.038s — She edits her own excitement in her head.

And suddenly, I'd feel silly for being excited. I'd read my own message again, like the extra excitement was a spelling mistake I could fix. Maybe I should've said less.

Focus: Embarrassed face then imagined correction. Down-right glance, text revision metaphor, clear it. Keep the comedy on her overthinking.

## 9. 104.038–117.158s — Neeko mattered beyond the time they shared.

I get attached to animals like that too. Neeko wasn't just a cat I looked after. I cared about her so much. Even after she found a family, that feeling didn't just switch off.

Focus: Neeko portrait and warm face. Canonical cream/ginger cat memory; no tragedy claim. Show the caring side of attachment.

## 10. 117.158–132.238s — Memories stay after life moves forward.

That's the thing. I remember people. I miss them. I carry little bits of them around, even when life has moved on. Sometimes I wish my brain had a slightly smaller storage plan.

Focus: Heart thread then one storage aside. Held warm metaphor then compact dry joke. Release tension without trivializing care.

## 11. 132.238–145.358s — A quiet reply does not define her worth.

I'm still figuring this out. I don't want to become someone who pretends not to care. I just want to stop treating every quiet reply like proof that I matter less.

Focus: Personal doubt and live correction. Matter-less text is crossed out, still-me lettering settles. Show a tentative choice rather than a cure.

## 12. 145.358–160.400s — She can care while keeping some care for herself.

I can miss someone and still put my phone down. I can be excited to see them without putting my whole day on hold. I'll probably always care a lot. I'd like to save a little of that for myself.

Focus: Resting phone then final face. Phone callback, room wide, gentle final portrait. Resolve the opening without a lecture.
