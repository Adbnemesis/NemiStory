# Story, interaction and visual direction

Use for new design and story/pacing/clarity revisions. The film must make sense without its creator explaining it beside the viewer. Apply the selected author's current character/production guides.

## One causal story

Save a concise direction brief:

- **Origin:** what caused this particular problem and why this character is here.
- **Want/stakes:** a concrete recognizable goal and why a wrong action or delay matters.
- **World rule:** the physical/story rules that make observation useful; show them before testing them.
- **Viewer role:** what looking reveals and what choosing changes in the authored film.
- **Choice → consequence → next need:** how each decision advances or complicates that same goal.
- **Payoff:** an unmistakable resolution of the original problem and an earned character-specific release.

For each obstacle, complete “because the last action did ___, we now need ___ to reach the original goal.” If it becomes an unrelated mission, revise/remove it. Re-establish the goal naturally through a persistent landmark, visible threat and motivated dialogue; do not repeatedly reintroduce the game. New mechanics must obey established rules or establish their rule visibly before the decision.

Keep an authoring state ledger: location/view sector; actor/support; threat progress; goal landmark; changed materials; viewer knowledge. This is not saved player state. Completed actions remain visibly true in following scenes. Avoid plot drift, duplicate unexplained threats and magic solutions introduced only after the choice.

## Fun, fair agency

Choose decisions to fit the premise and runtime; there is no mandatory puzzle count. Ask viewers to notice evidence, make a simple inference and commit. Distinguish alternatives through shape, material or behaviour. Explain enough stakes to make the choice meaningful without immediately narrating the answer.

Give prompt, physical, action-specific feedback. Wrong attempts can be funny surprises, expressive reactions or useful reveals of the rule. Keep recovery inexpensive: name/show the valid retry/safe number and continue the same story. Avoid long repeated lectures, punishing inspection or unexplained dead ends. Success needs a visible material result and progress toward the goal, not just “correct!” text.

Plan direct success, every wrong/retry path and passive straight-through viewing. Native seeking cannot know the previous route: every reachable entry must be coherent without inventory/history. Use self-contained authored states, landmarks and motivated rewinds where appropriate. Do not reset everything at every decision as a substitute for continuity.

## First-use instructions

Demonstrate the actual input early inside the predicament; avoid a detached long tutorial. Show and say:

1. **Look:** desktop click-and-drag; swipe in a supported YouTube phone app. Mention desktop WASD only when verified.
2. **Choose:** focus/click the desktop player, then press the number beside the desired action. On phones, use the matching numbered chapter if that channel/app provides it; description timestamps are recovery.
3. **Resume:** after pausing to inspect, use Play/K to see the result. A number is not an automatic unpause.
4. **Recover:** the offered retry/safe number, plus restart when useful.

Use concise specific labels such as “4 OPEN THE HATCH.” Put them close to actual objects at readable contrast/size, clear of seams, poles, faces and captions. Keep spoken number, label, chapter and destination identical. Never require precision timeline scrubbing as the main mechanic.

Dragging rotates the viewpoint; it does not drag a prop. If the story says the viewer folds/pulls/repairs something, a number selects that preauthored consequence. Drawn labels/captions are not tappable object controls. Do not ask viewers to perform custom key combos, timed reactions or gestures the ordinary player cannot detect.

## Brisk conversational flow

Write connected observation, reaction and intention in the author's voice. Read aloud, then listen to the actual recording for repetitive exposition, flat delivery and slow explanatory pivots. Energy comes from a clear next thought, physical progression and varied acting; speed and cut frequency alone do not create it.

Budget each beat from measured final dialogue, useful inspection, action and result. Keep transitions connected. Accidental multi-second tails, long “waiting for input” screens and chapter recaps are failures. Roughly0.5–1.5 seconds between linked spoken thoughts is a useful starting range, not a quota. Inspect ordinary gaps above about2 seconds and justify them as an actual joke, emotion, reading need or meaningful action. Users may voluntarily pause; a fixed movie cannot wait for their response.

Do not rush instructions into unintelligibility or blanket-trim silence. Preserve breaths, tension, dry holds and relief. Shorten/rewrite weak material before forcing speech to fit. Faster audio requires a shorter/restructured duration and rebuilt percentage map when appropriate, rather than restoring empty tails in old slots.

## Props viewers can see and understand

Inspect real rectilinear projections at ordinary FOV, not only the wall canvas or complete equirectangular strip. Hero props need recognizable silhouettes and enough visible surface to infer their role. Floor-level objects can fall below the normal horizon: add a readable top/face, edge thickness, supports/rails or a motivated closer look while keeping the actual support plane fixed.

Show the precise clue: unfinished span, crease, loose fastener, exposed contact, intact material. Use material-specific contours, restrained shadows and clear contact. A label cannot replace a bridge, sheet, exit or location. Keep actor/set scale credible; inspect soles/support, grips, authored pickup/release paths and both sides of contact ownership. Root motion alone is not walking or boarding.

Add purposeful context props that explain place, scale and cause. Keep them subordinate and retain them across connected views. Avoid empty interchangeable rooms and clue-obscuring clutter. Develop one audience focus at a time. Use held art, live revision, prop contact, scenery, camera or stillness according to the thought; completed ink stays still. VFX/SFX need named events, finite lifetimes and a perceivable story purpose. Extra effects cannot compensate for invisible evidence or flat acting.

## Continuous space and story

Keep each option and its result in a consistent sector. Native number seeking preserves viewpoint and pause; it cannot face a selected object automatically. Make results discoverable from every initial orientation with short stable direction cues and persistent evidence, without masking the action.

At successful result→next thought, preserve actual actor endpoint/scale, sector, support and a completed landmark. Carry the eye toward the next obstruction. Avoid new-round cards, forced front resets, spinning the view or unexplained teleportation. Establish genuine location changes with a reveal, motivated match cut, visible traversal or clear transition. A cut is not an animated walk.

## Cold-viewer review

Give a reviewer the film and its visible instructions without the source map or creator hints. Can they explain the origin, present goal, control, reason for the consequence and recovery? Can they find/distinguish the real props at normal width? Does each success move toward the original payoff? Where do they wait without learning, feeling or doing anything?

Rewrite the film when answers are unclear. External explanatory notes do not repair its first-use experience. A script/storyboard review can expose logic problems but is not a real player playthrough. Record exactly what was reviewed; leave unavailable perceptual tests pending.
