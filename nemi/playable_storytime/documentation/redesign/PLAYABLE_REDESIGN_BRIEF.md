# Nemi playable storytime — creative reset

Concept approved by the user on 26 October 2026 (“yea go ahead i like”). The first 40-second voice/storyboard audition is ready under `episodes/episode_002/`; the new full production remains at this creative review gate.

The plan below records the agreed direction. R9 and earlier source/media remain preserved, and the main playable player still points to R9. New audition material uses the separate episode_002 folder and a clearly labeled local audition player with 1/2 choice controls.

## Confirmed direction

- One upload, entirely inside YouTube.
- Keep 360° exploration and number-key interaction. These are the parts the user liked.
- Replace the confusing premise and weak challenges; keep Nemi recognizable.
- Use natural conversation, clear instructions and meaningful, visible consequences.
- Broaden editing/compositing beyond Godot where it improves the film. This user instruction supersedes earlier renderer-only restrictions for this redesign; original systems remain read-only.
- More purposeful VFX, sound and visual action. Preserve the existing paper/ink identity and avoid continuous unrelated clutter.
- Plan the experience before another expensive full render. The previous model of full movie delivery with listening still pending did not establish whether the script worked for the user.

## What went wrong

The current film asks the viewer to understand a volunteer-form story, remember which minor object matters, explore the room, interpret numbered routes and repeatedly request the next scene. Several decisions are arbitrary distractions rather than actions with clear stakes. Synchronization fixes solved the input defect; they did not establish an enjoyable game.

Longer sentences alone did not solve this. The story needs an immediate want, a visible obstacle, a reason to inspect the space and a consequence worth watching. Editing must express those events. A larger count of pose/VFX cues is not a creative acceptance test.

## Recommended story: Please Get Me Out of My Own Drawing

An explicitly fictional comedy/suspense story. Nemi is inside an unfinished sketchbook environment. A giant eraser has already removed a strip of the floor. She needs to reach the edge of the page. Her confidence in a world she drew collides with the shortcuts she took while drawing it. The viewer becomes the observant friend who sees the problem before she does.

The promise is understandable in the default forward view: **Nemi + an eraser tip removing the floor + a glimpse of the page edge.** Establish that event before asking the viewer to rotate. The eraser can then travel around the set. It is a character in the action, not a particle effect layered behind narration. The threat is playful and non-graphic.

Three principal decisions:

1. **Find a real route.** A convincing drawn door is flat; a torn paper opening casts a real gap/shadow. Labels: `1 · TRY THE DOOR` / `2 · TAKE THE TEAR`. The first decision teaches the input through the story. The door failure has a physical joke; the tear outcome visibly opens a path.
2. **Cross the missing floor.** One drawn bridge is unfinished; a loose paper tab can fold into a supported crossing. Labels: `4 · RUN ACROSS` / `5 · FOLD THE TAB`. Observation identifies the missing support. The successful action changes the environment, rather than awarding a text badge.
3. **Use the threat.** The eraser is travelling toward the page edge. Labels: `7 · RACE IT` / `8 · RIDE IT`. Racing fails when it removes the path; riding it turns the pursuing object into the escape vehicle. This is a proposed physical-comedy payoff, subject to a contact/blocking proof before it is scripted as finished animation.

The ending reaches the creator's studio outside the sketchbook. Nemi is relieved and faintly offended by how much planning her own drawing required. End on one physical callback to the unfinished exit, then stop. Do not add a fresh unexplained danger that cancels the success.

These are preliminary beats. During the storyboard, check that each alternative is visually plausible, each clue is readable, and the correct route is inferred from evidence rather than an explanatory speech. If the eraser ride cannot be staged convincingly, replace that action at the board stage rather than faking contact.

## Experience grammar

**Look → notice one relevant detail → press one of two displayed numbers → see a physical consequence.** Repeat that grammar, not a stock shot, joke, pose or instruction sentence.

- There are three main decisions. No separate start/practice key and no forward/continue keys between successful beats.
- Keep each choice attached to its object/action. The number, object silhouette and action name agree.
- Show at most two current actions. Never present all ten destinations as the primary interface.
- Speak one concise instruction at the decision. Let the picture carry the repeated labels.
- A selected destination begins with the matching action/result in the same environment. The result is a visible event and a brief confirmation, not a 3.5-second card replacing Nemi.
- Preserve the viewing direction. Keep Nemi's location/landmark consistent, and make important result changes visible near the selected object. Peripheral direction cues guide recovery when needed.
- Give a stable, readable observation window. The in-story eraser motion is authored movie time; it does not measure reaction speed or detect whether a viewer paused.
- A wrong route supplies an entertaining consequence, a short authored rewind and an obvious retry/safe option. Wrong choices must be worth exploring.
- If a viewer does nothing, the film shows Nemi's failed attempt, rewind and better attempt as a coherent time-loop story. Do not claim she received a choice the viewer never made.

## Provisional single-upload map

Use 240 seconds only as a planning candidate, with the main successful route around 168 seconds. The original brief permits adjusting length for pacing/quality. Final duration is chosen from the voiced animatic and then locked. Every chosen digit must be recalculated if it changes.

YouTube digits seek to a percentage of the full file. Editing freedom does **not** remove that constraint. Here, only six digits are choice destinations. The scenes span either 24 or 48 seconds, so the writing no longer has to imitate ten equal mini-scenes.

| Movie time | Story material | Shown choice / consequence |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:24 | Eraser hook, one goal, first scan | `1 door / 2 tear`; first instruction taught here |
| 0:24–0:48 | Door attempt, physical joke, short rewind, restored clue | Bad route 1; recovery points to 2 |
| 0:48–1:36 | Tear opens, Nemi advances, missing-floor problem develops | Safe route 2; next decision `4 run / 5 fold` |
| 1:36–2:00 | Bridge fails, reaction, short rewind | Bad route 4; recovery points to 5 |
| 2:00–2:48 | Paper tab unfolds, crossing, eraser changes the problem | Safe route 5; next decision `7 race / 8 ride` |
| 2:48–3:12 | Racing removes the path, reaction, short rewind | Bad route 7; recovery points to 8 |
| 3:12–4:00 | Eraser ride, escape into studio, earned comic payoff | Safe route 8 and ending |

Direct route: `start → 2 → 5 → 8`. Three decisions, three presses. A passive viewer sees all the failed and successful attempts. Keys 3/6/9 remain normal native percentage seeks, landing within continuous scenes; they are not advertised as extra game actions. Key 0 retains YouTube's ordinary restart behavior.

At the native level this remains authored navigation, not a game engine embedded inside YouTube. No inventory, score, memory, automatic answer grading, stateful branching or custom keyboard callbacks are promised.

## Spatial plan and 360 editing

The viewer's eye movement is part of the game. Establish the location before asking them to search it.

- **Front anchor:** Nemi and a persistent recognizable sketchbook landmark. Dialogue reactions remain readable when facing her.
- **Comfortable side sectors:** the two alternatives, usually within a deliberate left/right turn rather than at opposite poles. Exact yaw depends on readable projection tests.
- **One motivated rear rediscovery:** after establishing its tip/action in the opening view, let the eraser travel behind Nemi, with an audible cue and a spoken invitation to follow it. Do not repeatedly hide essential instructions behind the viewer.
- **Horizon band:** action labels, object silhouettes and evidence. Keep critical content away from poles/seams.
- **Across a jump:** retain room geometry and the selected object's orientation where the narrative permits. Show the local consequence there; use restrained peripheral return cues, not a forced camera reset.

Use edited layers in the spherical world: paper tears, ink impacts, environmental wipes, object animation, reaction illustrations and sound. A conventional flat closeup pasted full-screen would discard the 360 experience; it is not the default treatment. Any framed memory/cutaway is an object/portal inside the sphere and preserves the viewer's orientation.

The compositor must map the finished layers back into a correct 2:1 equirectangular movie. Check every effect from front/left/right/back, across seams and near the horizon. Avoid constant spins, global shake, rapid full-field flashes or blur that obscures the clue. Cinematic energy should come primarily from authored object/character action and cuts with stable spatial anchors.

## VFX and sound jobs

| Beat | Visual treatment | What it tells the viewer | Sound/performance |
|---|---|---|---|
| First 3 seconds | Eraser removes an actual strip of drawn floor; paper debris settles | The threat and goal are real inside the fiction | Existing recorded scrape/impact; Nemi notices before her body recoils |
| Door test | Door compresses into a flat ink shape; bounded impact accents | This exit was never constructed | Short physical accent, eye contact and a dry recovery |
| Tear route | Layered page curl and parallax reveal a readable gap | This route actually opens | Paper movement and a brief relief reaction |
| Missing bridge | A clear insert-in-space exposes the unfinished segment | There is a specific problem to solve | Nemi's embarrassed realization, then room to inspect |
| Tab choice | An authored fold becomes a grounded crossing; completed ink stays still | The viewer's chosen action has changed the scene | Finite paper/foot contact cues; no generic success chime as sole feedback |
| Wrong route reset | Brief scratch/rewind transition restores the same landmarks | This was a failed attempt; try another action | Recognizable reverse/edit cue from available licensed recordings; no fake state counter |
| Eraser payoff | Prop trajectory and Nemi's contact make the escape legible | The threat becomes useful | Anticipation, contact, travel, landing and a quiet aftermath |

Do not commit to a particular recording or claim it is licensed until its existing catalog/hash/provenance is checked. Do not synthesize replacement SFX. Music can build tension in selected passages and drop out for a joke; no metronomic bed in every scene. Preserve the cream paper, readable warm ink, canonical auburn/sage character and selective accents.

## Spoken tone sketch — not a locked script

> “Okay, that eraser just took out the floor. I'm on that floor. I drew this place, so you'd think I'd know how to leave. Look around for a way off the page, then press the number beside it. Please pick something I actually finished drawing.”

The opening has to be timed against the actual performance, not assumed to fit from word count. Start with the visible event, not a control lecture. The shorter drops punctuate a connected thought; the film must not speak in route names, inventory updates or three equally spaced fragments per scene.

Write a complete conversational scene before splitting recording takes. Keep the exact approved Sohee identity/settings. Reject awkward wording, pronunciation, overlong pauses and flat delivery during a short listening audition. Tempo and explicit quiet-gap edits may refine a good take; they do not rescue a weak performance. Rebuild all dependent timing after voice edits.

## Production plan

1. **Story and interaction board.** Save the one-sentence goal, three challenges, evidence, both consequences, recovery and ending. A first-time viewer should be able to describe the goal and the input without reading a manual.
2. **Voice-first audition.** Produce 30–45 seconds covering the hook, an emotional turn, one instruction and its payoff. Let the user hear this before making another full film. The voice is the central quality gate, not a listening-pending footnote after delivery.
3. **Voiced rough storyboard.** Build the complete direct and passive paths with simple spatial art and real voice. Test comprehension and pacing. No expensive polished VFX are needed to discover a confusing goal.
4. **Native YouTube control proof.** Before full production, test a small single-upload 360 proof with the intended percentage navigation, chapters, paused choices and preserved orientation on actual YouTube. Use an authorized unlisted workflow when available; a local player does not establish this. Record device/browser and results. If upload access is unavailable, keep that gate explicitly pending.
5. **Character and layer proof.** Use canonical Nemi performances and authored contact. Prove any new alpha/layer export and the eraser-ride contact over a short excerpt before promising the scene. This is new adapter work, not an already delivered capability.
6. **Final 360 edit/composite.** Assemble scene-space layers, finite VFX, text and recorded SFX from a source-bound edit manifest. Keep each input, timing, projection and export hash reproducible. Recheck exact duration/digit destinations after the final edit.
7. **Complete 1080p review.** Deliver the entire current cut; test all routes and listen to the complete soundtrack. User satisfaction is required before 4K. The approval receipt must bind the final composited movie, edit manifest and all picture/voice/sound inputs, not merely the underlying Godot plate.

Existing Godot/canonical rigs, FFmpeg/FFprobe and local Python imaging/audio libraries can support this workflow. A dedicated editor can be used if available or requested, but do not present unavailable software as installed. The current FFmpeg build lacks drawtext/subtitles filters; use existing stage text or separately rendered transparent text plates. Original storytime/Shorts sources remain read-only. All new work belongs inside `nemi/playable_storytime/`.

## Acceptance before full production

- The viewer identifies the one goal from the opening image and dialogue.
- The first real interaction is understood during that scene, without a separate instruction sequence.
- Each pair of alternatives has visual evidence and a predictable kind of consequence.
- Each displayed number reaches the exact matching action, including while paused or facing away.
- There are three choices on the successful route; no chain of intermediate continue buttons.
- Wrong routes are entertaining events, not reprimands or blank waiting rooms.
- Passive playback is coherent because rewinds explicitly connect failure to another attempt.
- Nemi's voice audition is heard and reviewed before the full render.
- The complete spatial rough cut makes sense before polishing effects.
- The final uploaded file receives its own YouTube/device verification; local success is reported separately.
