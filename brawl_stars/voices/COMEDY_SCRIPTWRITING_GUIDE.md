# Brawl Stars Comedy Scriptwriting Bible
### *The Gumbino & Solid jj Method for Long-Form YouTube Animated Videos*

> **The Core Premise**: This comedy style lives in the gap between **epic video game lore/grandeur** and **mundane, toxic internet gamer reality**. A mythical legendary assassin chameleon (Leon) acts like a high-strung, fast-talking kid desperately trying to justify terrible plays; an edgy assassin (Edgar) acts like a burnt-out, completely unbothered gamer who just wants the match to end. Across a 3–8 minute long-form YouTube episode, this dynamic sustains full narrative arcs, escalating chaos, running callbacks, and comedic character unraveling.

---

## 1. The Anatomy of the Comedy (The Formula)

In classic character comedy animation (such as the Solid jj and Gumbino style dialogue dynamic), the comedy succeeds because of **three strict comedic pillars**:

### Pillar 1: High Stakes Lore vs. Low Energy Gamer Arithmetic
- **The Lore expectation**: *"I am a Legendary Bird! I can freeze things with a flap of my wings!"*
- **The Gamer reality**: *"But you're level 50, I'm pretty sure the main legendary is level 70. Yeah, I'm not using my Master Ball on you."*
- **In Brawl Stars**: 
  - *Lore*: Leon is an elite invisible spirit assassin from the enchanted forest.
  - *Gamer Reality*: Leon is sitting in a corner bush for 2 minutes straight, terrified of a 14-cube Frank, waiting for 2nd place trophies.

### Pillar 2: The Con-Artist vs. The Unbothered Cynic (Dynamic Duo)
The comedy **requires** two contrasting energies:

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐        ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│        THE MANIC AGITATOR (LEON)             │   vs   │          THE DEADPAN CYNIC (EDGAR)           │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────┤        ├──────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ • Fast-talking sales pitch                   │        │ • Slow, flat, unbothered gamer reality       │
│ • Desperate rationalizations                 │        │ • Blunt arithmetic & factual roasts          │
│ • Escalates from pleading to screaming       │        │ • Barely raises his voice above room volume  │
│ • Breaks composure completely                │        │ • Long pauses before delivering 3-word burns │
│ • Example: "TRUST ME! I'M TELLING YOU!"      │        │ • Example: "He died."                        │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘        └──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Pillar 3: The Degradation of Dignity (Rule of Three)
The agitator never just gives up; their dignity deteriorates in 3 distinct steps:
1. **The Rational Argument**: *"I'm the only legendary Pokémon you've seen so far. Trust me!"*
2. **The Shameless Con**: *"Listen, between me and you, we get a new Master Ball every region..."*
3. **Total Loss of Pride / The Shameless Truth**: *"There's a level 70 Mewtwo after this, I lied just to get you to waste your Master Ball for no reason. I'm just a pre-legendary kid, sorry."*

## 2. Long-Form YouTube Narrative Architecture (3–8 Minute Episodes)

Unlike short-form content where a single joke ends at 45 seconds, long-form YouTube comedy animation (Gumbino, Solid jj, TerminalMontage) works like an escalating sitcom episode. It is structured into **4 distinct Narrative Acts**, driven by multiple conversational micro-beats and running gags.

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                             LONG-FORM EPISODE TIMELINE (3–8 MINUTES)                        │
├───────────────────────┬───────────────────────────┬───────────────────────┬─────────────────┤
│    ACT 1 (0:00-1:30)  │     ACT 2 (1:30-4:30)     │   ACT 3 (4:30-6:30)   │ ACT 4 (6:30-8+) │
│  "The Petty Debate &  │ "The Escalating Catastrophe│  "The Climax of Chaos │  "The Defeat    │
│    The Terrible Plan" │  & Guest Entanglement"   │   & Total Collapse"   │   Post-Mortem"  │
└───────────────────────┴───────────────────────────┴───────────────────────┴─────────────────┘
```

### The 4-Act Story Engine

#### Act 1: The Petty Debate & The Terrible Plan (0:00 – 1:30)
- **Objective**: Hook the audience with an intensely relatable, petty gaming dilemma (e.g. crouching in a bush with 100 HP, trying to push rank 30 at 3:00 AM, arguing over who carries the gems).
- **The Dynamic**: Leon pitches an insanely overcomplicated, mathematically doomed plan. Edgar tries to talk him down using cold, factual logic.
- **The Inciting Incident**: Leon acts anyway, triggering the first minor failure (e.g. accidentally throwing the ball to the enemy, or shooting a box and revealing their location).

#### Act 2: The Escalating Domino Effect & Guest Entanglement (1:30 – 4:30)
- **Objective**: The consequences compound exponentially. Instead of cutting to the end, the characters are stuck dealing with the aftermath.
- **Guest Brawler Interference**: A third or fourth brawler enters the dynamic (e.g., Crow swoops in to mock them; Colt shows up completely delusional thinking he's the main character; an enemy Frank or Bull is slowly marching toward them).
- **The Planted Running Gag**: Establish a comedic rule or object that will pay off later (e.g. Leon insisting his gadget clone has feelings; Edgar repeatedly checking his trophy loss calculation).
- **Dignity Breakdown**: Leon's composure cracks from confident con-artist to desperate rationalizations (*"NO—LOOK, HE DIDN'T SEE US! HE'S JUST WALKING THIS WAY BY ACCIDENT!"*).

#### Act 3: The Climax of Pure Chaos (4:30 – 6:30)
- **Objective**: Maximum comedic tension where all storylines collide.
- **The Execution**: Every character uses their Super or gadget simultaneously in the worst possible way. Friendly fire, misdirected jumps, toxic pin spamming while taking lethal damage, and screaming panic.
- **Vocal Extremes**: Leon reaches peak manic hysteria (`screaming_panic`); Edgar drops to absolute rock-bottom apathy (`deadpan` / quiet cynical realization).

#### Act 4: The Defeat Screen & The Awkward Post-Mortem (6:30 – 8:00+)
- **Objective**: The aftermath. The action stops dead.
- **The Iconic Awkward Pause**: 2–3 seconds of pure silence or awkward match defeat music while both characters stare ahead in stunned numbness.
- **The Callback Punchline**: Edgar brings back the exact argument from Act 1 with flat, unbothered brutality.
  - *Edgar*: *"Minus twelve trophies."*
  - *Leon*: *"...I had the angle."*
  - *Edgar*: *"You hit the post on an empty net."*

---

### The Secret Weapon of Long-Form: The "Micro-Scene Loop"
In a 5-minute video, viewers will leave if two characters just talk in one spot without progression. Structure the video into **3 to 4 modular scene blocks** (each 60–90 seconds) mapped to production JSON files:

```
episode_01/
├── scene_01_spawn_bush.json      (Act 1: The Argument & The Plan)
├── scene_02_mid_scuffle.json     (Act 2A: The First Failure & Crow Enters)
├── scene_03_hypercharge_boss.json(Act 2B / Act 3: The Climax & Chaos)
└── scene_04_defeat_lobby.json    (Act 4: The Post-Mortem & Running Gag Payoff)
```

### The Power of the Awkward Pause
In short-form, every second must be packed with audio. **In long-form YouTube comedy, silence is 50% of the joke.**
- After an absurd statement, insert **1.5 to 2.5 seconds of dead silence** or a quiet blinking SFX before Edgar replies.
- The contrast between Leon talking at 220 words per minute and Edgar waiting 2 full seconds to say three words (*"...He died."*) is the core engine of internet comedy.

## 3. Writing for Qwen3-TTS (Formatting Codes)

To get hilarious, human-sounding comedic delivery out of the TTS model, **you must write the text specifically for the acoustic prosody engine**:

### Rule 1: Never write more than 12 words in a single sentence.
- ❌ *Bad*: *"I am telling you that if we run out into the middle right now we are going to be completely fine because I have my gadget ready to go."* (TTS becomes an audiobook narrator).
- ✅ *Good*: *"Trust me! I jump in, I hit gadget, free trophies!"* (Punchy, fast-paced, human).

### Rule 2: Use Ellipses (`...`) for Stunned Processing Time.
- In Qwen3-TTS, an ellipsis forces the speaker to take a subtle breath and hesitate, mimicking an authentic human "double-take":
- Example: `"...why?"` or `"Wait... are you serious right now?"`

### Rule 3: Use Em Dashes (`—`) for Abrupt Shouts and Cuts.
- A mid-sentence em dash snaps the pitch upward into panicked urgency:
- Example: `"NO—WHAT ARE YOU DOING?!"` or `"PFFT—bro, there's no way."`

### Rule 4: Use ALL CAPS Exclusively for Composure Breakdown.
- Save ALL CAPS for when Leon or Edgar genuinely snaps:
- Example: `"I WON'T DIE! WATCH ME!"`

### Rule 5: Use Conversational Stutters & Repetition.
- Comedic timing relies on imperfect speech:
- `"Wait, wait, wait."` (rhythmic caution) vs. `"Waitwaitwait."` (rapid panic).

## 5. Long-Form Episode Architecture (Full 3–5 Minute Workflow)

For YouTube long-form videos, an episode is not a single audio file—it is a sequence of **Modular Scene Chapters**. This keeps Godot animation timelines organized, allows camera angles and backgrounds to cut cleanly, and lets you re-generate single lines without touching the entire video.

### Recommended Project Directory Structure:
```
brawl_stars/
└── episodes/
    └── ep01_brawl_ball_meltdown/
        ├── scenes/
        │   ├── scene_01_kickoff_debate.json   (Act 1: The Plan & First Mistake)
        │   ├── scene_02_overtime_panic.json    (Act 2: Mortis Respawns & Crow Arrives)
        │   ├── scene_03_hypercharge_climax.json(Act 3: Triple Super Meltdown)
        │   └── scene_04_defeat_lobby.json      (Act 4: The Silent Post-Mortem)
        ├── audio/                              (Generated WAV files)
        │   ├── s01_01.wav
        │   ├── s01_02.wav
        │   └── ...
        └── episode_manifest.json               (Full episode sequence & timing)
```

## 6. Long-Form YouTube Retention Rules

To keep viewers watching an animated comedy sketch across 3 to 8 minutes, follow these four production rules:

### 1. The 5-Second Cold Open Hook (In Media Res)
- **Never start with a quiet intro or casual setup.**
- Start immediately in the middle of chaos or right as an absurd argument peaks:
  - *Edgar*: *"Why are you holding the ball? Their whole team is dead, just walk it in."*
  - *Leon*: *"Because it's not stylish! Winning without a trickshot is basically losing!"*

### 2. The Rhythm Ladder (Avoid Audio Monotony)
If Leon screams for 4 minutes straight, the audience gets ear fatigue. If Edgar is deadpan for 4 minutes straight, the video feels boring. **Alternate rhythm constantly**:
```
[High Energy Fast-Talk] (Leon 15s) 
       ▼
[Blunt 3-Word Reality] (Edgar 3s) 
       ▼
[Awkward Dead Silence / Eye Blink] (1.5s Pause)
       ▼
[Sudden Explosive Shock / Loud SFX] (Crow/Bull 5s)
       ▼
[Stunned Breathless Whisper] (Leon 2s)
```

### 3. The Running Callback (Payoff in the Defeat Screen)
Plant a throwaway detail in Act 1 that pays off in Act 4:
- **Act 1 (0:45)**: Leon boasts: *"My trickshot angle has a 99.4% probability factor. I did the geometry."*
- **Act 2 (2:30)**: Leon fails, blames a piece of grass: *"The grass physics in this map are clearly bugged."*
- **Act 4 (4:15)**: Edgar and Leon sit silently in the defeat lobby. Edgar pulls up the match stats:
  - *Edgar*: *"The geometry missed the goal by twenty-four meters."*
  - *Leon*: *"...The grass was slippery."*
  - *Edgar*: *"We were on concrete."*

## 7. How to Render Long-Form Scripts

Run the universal generator on your episode's scene files:

```bash
# Render a specific scene block
/Users/talus/Documents/adb/.venv/bin/python brawl_stars/voices/generate_brawler.py \
  --script brawl_stars/episodes/ep01_brawl_ball_meltdown/scenes/scene_01_kickoff_debate.json

# Or render an entire batch across the full episode
for scene in brawl_stars/episodes/ep01_brawl_ball_meltdown/scenes/*.json; do
  /Users/talus/Documents/adb/.venv/bin/python brawl_stars/voices/generate_brawler.py --script "$scene"
done
```

The system will:
1. Automatically route lines to the appropriate base voice actor (`leon` or `edgar`).
2. Apply pitch shifts (e.g. Crow `-2.0`, Colt `+2.0`, Bull `-4.0`).
3. Inject the exact acting style prompts.
4. Strip vocoder delay artifacts automatically via `strip_boundary_bleed`.
5. Save clean, 24kHz normalized WAV files directly into your episode audio folders ready for Godot!
