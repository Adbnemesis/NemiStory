## 1. Core Dialogue Philosophy: Spoken, Not Written

Nemi scripts must sound like **someone talking to a friend across a kitchen table**, never like someone reading a written essay aloud into a microphone.

```
WRITTEN LANGUAGE (ESSAY)                   SPOKEN LANGUAGE (NEMI)
┌───────────────────────────────────────┐  ┌───────────────────────────────────────┐
│ "I encountered substantial difficulty │  │ "Wait—no, because I actually tried   │
│ when attempting to animate my first   │  │ to draw this frame twenty times, and  │
│ walk cycle due to anatomical factors."│  │ every single time... it just looked   │
│                                       │  │ like she was slipping on an invisible │
│                                       │  │ banana peel. It was horrifying."      │
└───────────────────────────────────────┘  └───────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### The Spoken Voice Principle
If a line looks grammatically pristine, well-balanced, and formally constructed on the page, **it is probably wrong for Nemi**. Natural speech is characterized by:
* Thought pivots and mid-sentence self-corrections
* Sentence fragments that trail off or restart
* Brief rhetorical check-ins with the listener
* Pauses where visual acting carries the meaning
* Concrete, visual sensory words over abstract concepts

## 2. Syntactic Building Blocks of Nemi's Speech

Nemi’s speech patterns are built from five signature conversational devices:

### 1. Mid-Sentence Self-Corrections (The "Actually, No" Pivot)
Nemi often speaks as her brain processes information in real time, catching inaccuracies or adjusting her self-presentation:
* *"I had a completely foolproof plan for this video. Well—not foolproof. Actually, it was mostly just an empty folder on my desktop and a dream."*
* *"I spent two hours working out. Okay, forty minutes working out, fifteen minutes stretching, and an hour trying to choose the right playlist."*

### 2. Conversational Fragments & Rapid Starters
She frequently starts sentences with natural spoken disrupters rather than formal subjects:
* *"Wait."*
* *"No, because listen—"*
* *"Okay, hold on."*
* *"Actually..."*
* *"Point is..."*
* *"Long story short..."*

### 3. Rhetorical Reality Checks
She draws the audience into her internal dilemmas by inviting them to validate her weirdness:
* *"Please tell me I'm not the only person who does this."*
* *"Would you have walked away? Exactly. Neither did I."*
* *"You know that feeling when you're completely confident right up until the exact second you have to perform?"*

### 4. The Two-Sentence Deadpan Setup and Drop
Nemi sets up an expectation with mild enthusiasm, pauses, and drops it with an understated factual observation:
* *"I was convinced that warming up for five minutes was plenty."*  
  *(Pause)*  
  *"My hamstrings disagreed."*
* *"I told myself I would only watch one episode before drawing."*  
  *(Pause)*  
  *"It was a twenty-four episode season."*

### 5. Conversational Re-Centering ("Anyway...")
After going on an impassioned tangent about anime lore, vocal acoustics, or gym mechanics, she catches herself and snaps back to the main point:
* *"Anyway... that is how I ended up sitting on the floor at midnight eating cold rice."*
* *"Moving on before I embarrass myself further."*

## 3. Vocabulary & Linguistic Tone

### Approved Vocal Register
* **Casual, Smart, and Colloquial**: Intelligent vocabulary used casually, without academic pretension (e.g., *"statistically impossible"*, *"catastrophic miscalculation"*, *"hypothetically"*).
* **Contractions are Mandatory**: Always use `I'm`, `didn't`, `wouldn't`, `can't`, `it's`, `there's`. Uncontracted forms (`do not`, `cannot`) are reserved strictly for emphatic or deadpan punchlines (*"I cannot legally defend this"*).
* **Restrained Modern Slang**: Uses natural modern conversational terms (*"fr"*, *"low-key"*, *"zero percent"*, *"crashout"*), but never spams ephemeral trends.

### Prohibited Vocabulary & Tropes
* 🚫 **No Generic Broadcaster Openers**: Never write *"Hey guys!"*, *"Welcome back to my channel!"*, *"What's up YouTube!"*.
* 🚫 **No Desperate Call-to-Action Begging**: Never write *"Smash that like button!"*, *"Don't forget to subscribe and hit the bell!"*.
* 🚫 **No Formal Essay Transitions**: Never write *"Furthermore"*, *"In addition"*, *"Henceforth"*, *"In conclusion"*.
* 🚫 **No Gender Self-Reference (Rule 7)**: Never write *"As a girl"*, *"Being a woman"*, *"She... I mean, I"*.

## 5. Nemi's Internal Monologue Voice

When Nemi speaks her thoughts aloud (internal monologue or voiceover reflection), her voice exhibits sharp observation, vulnerability, and micro-escalation:

### Example 1: The Creative Trap
> *"The sketch looks fine. It’s clean. The silhouette is readable. But what if the line weight on the cuff of the sleeve is three pixels too thick? Nobody will notice. Literally not one human on Earth will care. ...Okay, I’m redrawing the whole arm."*

### Example 2: The Social Calculation
> *"Someone in the elevator just asked me how my day was going. Normal human response: 'Good, thanks, how about you?' My brain’s response: calculate the exact atmospheric pressure in the cabin, forget how breathing works, and reply: 'Yes.' ...Cool. Nailed it."*

### Example 3: The Athletic Illusion
> *"The weight on the barbell looks lighter from a distance. That's a scientific fact. When you're standing ten feet away, it looks like foam. It’s only when you put your hands on the knurling that you realize Isaac Newton is personally holding it down."*

## 6. Deadpan Delivery Mechanics in Dialogue

Deadpan requires precise rhythmic orchestration between words, silences, and subtitles.

```
PHASE 1: THE HOPEFUL SETUP
"I was pretty sure the export was going to take five minutes."
                 │
                 ▼
PHASE 2: THE REALITY CHECK (SILENCE / HOLD)
[Hold on static face for 1.5 seconds. Blink once.]
                 │
                 ▼
PHASE 3: THE UNDERSTATED DROP
"...It took seven hours."
                 │
                 ▼
PHASE 4: THE RESIGNATION
"...And the audio was muted."
```

### Script Notation for Deadpan
In Nemi scripts, deadpan moments should be explicitly formatted with bracketed timing and stage directions:

```text
NEMI:
"I double-checked the camera settings before recording the entire twenty-minute story."

[PAUSE — 1.8s. NEMI BLINKS TWICE. EYES LOCK ON VIEWER.]

NEMI:
"...I had the lens cap on."

[SUBTITLE CLEARS. HOLD STILLNESS FOR 1.0s.]

NEMI:
"...Anyway."
```

## 7. The 12-Point AI Scriptwriter Checklist

Before approving any line of dialogue generated for Nemi, verify against these twelve mandatory checks:

1. **Does this sound spoken?** (If read aloud, does it flow naturally with breaths and contractions?)
2. **Does this sound like Nemi?** (Is it observant, self-aware, and slightly chaotic?)
3. **Is the hook immediate?** (Does the first line grab curiosity within 3 seconds without boilerplate?)
4. **Is context introduced briskly?** (Does the premise land in under 15 seconds?)
5. **Is there genuine escalation?** (Does the situation progress from minor oddity to compounding dilemma?)
6. **Is there room for visual acting?** (Does the dialogue leave pauses for reactions and doodles?)
7. **Do jokes arrive naturally through delivery?** (Are punchlines grounded in honest reactions rather than setup/punchline joke formulas?)
8. **Are silences and deadpan beats preserved?** (Are pauses written as distinct beats, not rushed?)
9. **Is the ending satisfying and understated?** (Does it avoid generic subscription begging?)
10. **Is Rule 7 strictly obeyed?** (Zero self-referential gender phrases like "as a girl" or third-person pronouns for herself?)
11. **Does it avoid generic influencer language?** (No "Hey guys!", no hype broadcasting?)
12. **Could this line be said by any generic cartoon character?** (If YES, discard and rewrite with Nemi's specific observational voice!)
