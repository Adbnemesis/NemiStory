# Common Storytime Animation Style
## Universal Direction, Visual Language, and Staging Standards for Illustrated Storytelling

**Document Status**: LOCKED & AUTHORITATIVE COMMON SPECIFICATION  
**Scope**: Shared Animation Language across all characters (Nemi, ADB, and future productions)  
**Location**: `docs/animation/COMMON_STORYTIME_ANIMATION_STYLE.md`

---

## 1. Foundational Philosophy

> **THE STORYTELLER PRINCIPLE**:  
> The viewer must always feel: *"A real person is telling a personal story through live, hand-drawn illustration."*  
> **NOT**: *"An animated puppet or VTuber is mechanically reciting a script."*

The purpose of animation in this medium is **NOT "continuous movement"**—it is **"visual storytelling that punctuates narration."** Every frame, movement, line, and silence exists solely to clarify, enhance, or humorously punctuate the spoken narration.

---

## 2. Hand-Drawn Visual Language & Linework

### Organic Linework Standard
* **Master Contour Color**: Deep charcoal sepia ink (`#2b2623`). Never use pure digital black (`#000000`).
* **Line Weight**: Standard range between `2.5px` and `4.2px` at 1080p canvas resolution.
* **Variable Line Weight**: Lines taper naturally at endpoints and swell slightly at corners, curves, and weight-bearing joints.
* **Intentional Imperfection**: Slight organic asymmetry, gentle pen lift tapers, and human pen strokes.
* **Authored vs. Sterile**:
  - **CORRECT**: Hand-drawn bezier paths with organic control points that reflect human hand movements.
  - **INCORRECT**: Perfect geometric primitives (pure CAD circles, sterile rects) or procedural noise/jitter overlays applied on top of sterile vectors.

### Color Palette Philosophy
* **Foundation**: Warm, paper-like backgrounds (`#faf6ee`, `#f5efe4`) providing an illustrated sketchbook feeling.
* **Character Harmonization**: Muted, sophisticated palettes with high contrast against the paper background, paired with vivid accent colors used strictly for emotional bursts (blush, anger marks, shock rays).
* **Negative Space**: At least 35% to 50% of the screen area should remain clear, allowing key drawings, props, and subtitles to breathe.

## 3. Visual Storytelling Hierarchy

When staging any beat or visual moment, adhere to this strict priority ranking:

```
1. Spoken Narration (The master clock: voice cadence, tone, punchline)
2. Character Body Acting (Posture shift, lean, weight transfer, gestures)
3. Facial Emotion (Eye contact, eyebrow tension, mouth shape, blush)
4. Meaningful Visual Illustration (Hand-drawn doodles, diagrams, metaphor sketches)
5. Props & Cutaways (Physical objects or complete illustrated cutaways)
6. Camera Reframing (Snap cuts, punch-zooms, asymmetric framing)
7. Expression FX (Authored reaction marks: sweat drops, shock lines)
8. Subtitles (Restrained, crisp <= 5 words per card)
9. SFX Punctuation (Crisp, selective audio cues supporting actions)
10. Ambient Staging (Subtle background details, negative space)
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **No lower-level element may ever distract from, cover, or conflict with a higher-level element.**

## 4. Visual Rhythm, Movement & Stillness

### The Power of the Comedic Hold
* In comedy and personal storytelling, **stillness is as powerful as movement**.
* When delivering an absurd statement, deadpan punchline, or realization, characters must **snap into a freeze** with **ZERO motion** for `0.4s` to `1.5s`.
* Do not apply idle bobbing, breathing loops, or procedural floating during holds.

### Snap Transitions vs. Slow Tweens
* Emotional shifts, punch-zooms, and reaction reveals should snap rapidly (`0.08s` to `0.20s` tween or instantaneous snap cut).
* Slow, floaty transitions destroy comedic timing and feel like PowerPoint presentations.

### Asymmetry in Posing
* Humans rarely sit or stand in perfect bilateral symmetry.
* Weight must always rest primarily on one leg or side of the torso.
* Shoulders should tilt at an angle counter to the pelvis.
* Gestures should feature one dominant acting arm while the secondary arm assumes a relaxed counter-pose.
