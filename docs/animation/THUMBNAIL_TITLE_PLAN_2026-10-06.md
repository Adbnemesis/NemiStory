## The weakness in our current set

The last redesign made readable images, but kept much of the same structure: a large Impact headline, a friendly/reaction portrait, then smaller illustrative props. Different background colours did not remove that structure. This is a visible creative diagnosis, not a proven causal explanation for the reported CTR.

- ADB's crush: the serious school friend is small beside a large smiling ADB and a heart. The actual confession feels secondary.
- Nemi's kitchen: “MOM WALKED IN” could describe thousands of stories. The distinguishing chicken/hairdryer gag is too small and the dryer is presented as a detached prop.
- Nemi's taxi: the portrait/headline dominate while the rear-end contact sits low and right. The bridge adds travel context but competes with the important event.
- Several images have similar face crops and direct-to-viewer gazes. The scene lacks another person's intention or an action for the face to respond to.
- The celebration and creator-view-count episodes ask strangers to care about a creator's milestones before they know the creator. Better packaging can sharpen these episodes, but does not give them the same broad premise as a confession, rescue or bizarre kitchen mistake.

## The new creative system

1. **Write the click promise first.** In one sentence: what happened, why it is unusual, and what the viewer wants explained. Use a real event from the script. Leave the payoff unresolved where it matters.
2. **Design the title and image together.** The title gives the relationship, context or unusual action. The image shows the most compelling moment. One short thumbnail headline adds a reaction, deadline or contradiction; it should not repeat the title verbatim.
3. **Stage a moment.** Choose the confession, the desperate defrost attempt, the rear-end impact or its aftermath. Use eye-lines, believable prop grip/contact, opposing intentions and a foreground event. The character can be large, but should be doing or witnessing something.
4. **Let the action lead.** Start with the scene without type. At 160×90, a reviewer should name the important object/action. If they only see “Nemi is shocked,” the scene needs work. Then add the single headline. Enlarge/reframe the real prop rather than redrawing it into a different style.
5. **Choose colour for hierarchy.** Separate face, action and background with value/colour contrast. A plain paper background can work. Use location detail only if it helps understand the event. Avoid automatic bridge/crowd/doodle filler.
6. **Choose type after composition.** Keep one phrase, usually 1–4 words as a starting heuristic, not a mandatory template. Try the existing Patrick Hand or Caveat Bold when their shapes remain legible; use Impact when a compact emphatic phrase benefits from it. No universal “best font.” Reduce banner-like text coverage so the scene gets room. Keep small labels out of the image.
7. **Match the film directly.** Use the exact narrator rig, historical/current ADB variant, supporting character art, line colour, fills and prop language shown in that episode. Compare a thumbnail crop beside an actual film frame. Stronger crop/acting is allowed through existing controls; a replacement face, painterly lighting or copied anime styling is not.
8. **Check the promise.** Confirm it against the actual script and opening. The thumbnail must not suggest a more intense or different story than the film delivers. Packaging work preserves the movie/voice; any needed change to an opening is a separate animation request.

White outline/aura remains optional, and should be subtle enough to support separation without replacing the sense of a shared scene. Doodles should explain force, attention or a thought. They need a purpose. No AI image generation or restyling; render editable static Godot compositions using existing assets and supported controls.

## Production and review sequence

- Confirm each pilot's actual uploaded pair and save it as the control. Preserve current deliveries/source/QA under `thumbnail/revisions/` before any future replacement.
- Save a brief for each challenger: source event, intended audience question, title, single phrase, main action, gaze/contact direction, source assets and what this option tests.
- Build the six editable Godot compositions. Reuse school support art, existing chicken/dryer props, travel car/seat/patrol art and supported rig controls. If a desired pose cannot be achieved cleanly, change staging rather than replacing the rig or face.
- Inspect full-size exports, 320×180 and 160×90, in a feed mockup with the actual proposed title beneath each image and a duration badge. Compare against the uploaded control and the references at equivalent thumbnail sizes. Check both light and dark surrounds.
- Run three diagnostic checks: can someone name the event without the headline; does the title add useful context; does the image look like a scene from this film? An informal viewer preference check is useful creative feedback, not a measured CTR forecast.
- Record dimensions, hashes, source art, render logs and what was actually viewed. Package source/delivery/review without changing movies, voices or libraries. Platform publication/test setup is a separate explicitly requested action.

## Test and decision plan

Use YouTube Studio's native concurrent title-and-thumbnail test where available: the actual current pair plus the two challengers. It can compare up to three pairs and may take up to two weeks. It chooses on watch time and may report the same performance or an inconclusive result. Avoid manual changes during the test because they stop it. Check account/video eligibility before setup; this plan does not imply our channels are eligible. [Official A/B testing guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400?hl=en).

First-round pair testing asks which complete package works better. It does not isolate whether the title or the drawing caused the difference. If a useful winner emerges and impressions permit, the next round can hold its title fixed to test image direction, or hold its image fixed to test wording.

Record per pilot: actual title/image revision, test dates/status, impressions, traffic-source breakdown, video CTR by relevant source where available, average view duration, first-30-second retention if available, and the native experiment's result/watch-time share. Variant CTR should be recorded only if actually provided; watch-time share is not CTR, and the video's blended CTR during an experiment is not a per-option result.

Judge progress on three linked outcomes: better click response among comparable viewers, a promise that retains those viewers, and resulting watch time/reach. An expanding audience can lower CTR while delivering more views. A 20% figure from a tiny or loyal audience would not establish 20% for broad Browse/Suggested impressions. Do not declare a winner after a handful of clicks, a fixed 24 hours, or a sequential before/after swap with different audiences.

If native testing is unavailable or impressions are too low, preserve that limitation. A documented sequential change is an observational comparison, not a controlled winner. Do not assign fake confidence or a universal sample threshold. Keep an inconclusive test labelled inconclusive and use the results to choose the next hypothesis.

For future episodes, choose a concrete cold-viewer premise before production and reserve a strong story moment for the thumbnail. Check that the opening immediately supports the promise; this connection is also recommended in [YouTube's content-performance guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16559650?hl=en). Existing movies remain outside this thumbnail/title plan's edit scope.
